XGB_predict_LLZO.py — How It Works
=====================================

OVERVIEW
--------
This script predicts the room-temperature ionic conductivity (S/cm) of
Li7La3Zr2O12 (LLZO) using an XGBoost model that combines three categories
of features:

  1. Composition-only features (~16 features) — derived purely from the
     chemical formula, no crystal structure required.
  2. Defect proxy features (3 features) — Li vacancy estimates and a
     combined defect strength, all derived from oxidation state guessing
     without a CIF.
  3. BVSE features (6 value features + 1 flag + 2 derived) — raw structural
     descriptors capturing Li-ion migration barriers, geometric
     accessibility, and pathway connectivity. All BVSE values are kept
     in raw eV units; no log or exp transforms are applied to these
     features.

The model is trained on 478 OBELiX experimental entries. The target
variable is log10(sigma), where sigma is ionic conductivity in S/cm.

Hyperparameters are tuned once via Optuna (200 trials, 5-fold CV with
early stopping) on the full feature set and cached to xgb_best_params.json.
Subsequent runs skip tuning entirely and load directly from the cache.


STEP-BY-STEP PIPELINE
----------------------

STEP 1 — Fetch LLZO structure from Materials Project
  The script connects to the Materials Project API (requires MP_API_KEY
  environment variable) and searches for Li7La3Zr2O12. If multiple
  entries are returned, it picks the one with the lowest energy above the
  convex hull (i.e., the most thermodynamically stable polymorph). The
  structure is saved as a CIF file (LLZO_mp.cif).

STEP 2 — Compute BVSE features for LLZO using bvlain
  The bvlain library (Lain) runs two calculations on the CIF:

  a) bvse_distribution: places the Li1+ ion on a fine 3D grid (0.2 Å
     resolution, 10 Å cutoff) and evaluates the bond-valence site energy
     at every grid point. This produces an energy landscape for Li hopping.

     From this landscape the following model features are derived:

       bvse_energy    — 3D percolation barrier (E_3D), the activation
                        energy Ea most directly linked to conductivity.
                        Kept in raw eV. One physical quantity, one
                        representation.

       topology_score — continuous connectivity score: dimensionality / 3.0
                        0.00 = no percolation, 0.33 = 1D only,
                        0.67 = 2D, 1.00 = 3D. Single scalar; no
                        binary or entropy variants.

       bvse_anisotropy — spread of all three percolation barriers:
                           max(E_1D, E_2D, E_3D) − min(E_1D, E_2D, E_3D)
                         A large value means conduction is much easier
                         in some directions than others. Units: eV.

       bvse_accessible — fraction of grid points whose bond-valence site
                         energy is within 1 eV of the global minimum; a
                         proxy for the density of available Li sites.

  b) void_distribution: computes the geometric free-sphere radius at
     every grid point (the radius of the largest sphere that fits without
     overlapping framework atoms).

       bvse_bottleneck — minimum free-sphere radius along the 3D
                         percolating void path (Å); smaller values mean
                         Li faces tighter geometric bottlenecks.
                         Kept raw (Å), not log-transformed.

  Additionally:
       bvse_li_count   — total Li atoms per unit cell (from the fetched
                         structure).
       bvse_available  — binary flag: 1 if BVSE was computed from a real
                         or proxy CIF, 0 if unavailable (all BVSE value
                         features zeroed out for that training entry).

  Derived BVSE features (each encodes one physical quantity):

       bvse_energy_inv      — 1 / (bvse_energy + 1e-6)
                              Reciprocal activation energy; captures the
                              exponential Arrhenius relationship between
                              Ea and conductivity without log-transforming
                              the feature itself.

       li_mobility_capacity — bvse_accessible × bvse_li_count
                              Joint proxy for available carrier count and
                              site accessibility; no Ea involved.

  Note: NO log or exp transforms are applied to any BVSE feature.
  All values are stored in their natural units (eV or Å or fractions).

STEP 3 — Extract composition and defect proxy features
  a) Composition features (~16): computed from the chemical formula alone
     using pymatgen. These mirror the features built in
     03_tier1_features.ipynb:

       li_count                — Li atoms per formula unit
       mean_electronegativity  — composition-weighted mean Pauling EN
       anion_electronegativity — EN of the most electronegative anion
       cation_anion_en_diff    — anion EN minus composition-weighted
                                 cation EN
       mean_ionic_radius       — composition-weighted mean ionic radius (Å)
       anion_ionic_radius      — ionic radius of the dominant anion (Å)
       radius_std              — standard deviation of ionic radii across
                                 all atoms in the formula
       anion_polarizability    — weighted mean anion polarizability
                                 (O: 1.2, S: 5.5, Se: 7.0, etc.)
       mean_atomic_mass        — composition-weighted mean atomic mass (amu)
       n_elements              — number of distinct elements
       mean_atomic_number      — composition-weighted mean Z
       anion_type_*            — one-hot encoding: oxide / sulfide /
                                 halide / mixed / other

     Anions are identified by matching elements against a known anion list
     (O, S, Se, Te, F, Cl, Br, I, N); if none match, the most
     electronegative element is treated as the anion.

  b) Defect proxy features (3): estimated from pymatgen oxidation state
     guessing, without needing a CIF:

       li_stoich_deviation — abs(li_actual − li_ideal), where li_ideal
                             is the Li count required to balance the
                             non-Li framework charge. Zero = perfectly
                             stoichiometric; positive = Li excess or
                             deficit.

       dopant_presence     — count of elements in the reduced formula
                             whose stoichiometric coefficient is
                             non-integer (> 0.05 from nearest integer),
                             used as a proxy for aliovalent substitution.

       defect_strength     — li_stoich_deviation + dopant_presence.
                             Combined scalar summarising total defect
                             load without duplicating individual terms.

STEP 4 — Load and prepare training data
  Training data comes from three CSV files:

    comp_train.csv              — identifiers and source labels
    comp_train_features.csv     — pre-computed composition features for
                                  all 478 OBELiX entries
    bvse_features_combined.csv  — BVSE features for the subset of entries
                                  that have CIF files

  The BVSE CSV is left-joined onto the training set. Entries without a CIF
  fall back through a source hierarchy:

    Source tier     Confidence   How BVSE was obtained
    -----------     ----------   ------------------------------------
    real            1.00         Original dataset CIF (best quality)
    real            0.85         Exact formula/chemsys match from MP
    proxy           0.05–0.75    Relaxed parent structure (minor dopants
                                 dropped); confidence = exp(-3 * lattice_score)
    guessed         0.00         No CIF available

  For entries in the guessed tier, all BVSE value features are set to 0.0
  and bvse_available is set to 0, letting the model learn to ignore BVSE
  features when no structural data exists.

  Sample weights are assigned by source tier and passed to XGBoost's fit():

    real    → weight 1.0   (high-confidence structural features)
    proxy   → weight 0.5   (approximate structural features)
    guessed → weight 0.1   (composition-only proxy; BVSE all zeros)

  Feature construction order:

    1. topology_score        — dimensionality / 3.0
    2. bvse_anisotropy       — max − min across all three barriers (eV)
    3. bvse_energy_inv       — 1 / (bvse_energy + 1e-6)
    4. li_mobility_capacity  — bvse_accessible × bvse_li_count

  No log or exp transforms are applied at any stage. All features are
  used at their raw scale.

  Li site counts are read directly from the CIF files on disk.

STEP 5 — Optuna hyperparameter tuning with early stopping in CV folds
  On the first run, Optuna searches for the best XGBoost hyperparameters
  using 200 trials of 5-fold cross-validation. Within each CV fold the
  training portion is further split 85/15; XGBoost trains on the 85%
  inner split and early-stops on the 15% inner validation split
  (30 rounds patience) before being scored on the outer held-out fold.
  This prevents n_estimators from being artificially inflated and gives
  a conservative, regularised estimate of each hyperparameter set's
  true generalisation error.

  The Optuna search space covers:

    n_estimators      100–1000    number of boosting rounds
    max_depth         2–8         depth per tree
    learning_rate     0.005–0.3   log-uniform shrinkage
    subsample         0.4–1.0     row subsampling per tree
    colsample_bytree  0.3–1.0     column subsampling per tree
    min_child_weight  1–20        minimum sum of instance weight per leaf
    reg_alpha         1e-8–10     L1 regularisation (log-uniform)
    reg_lambda        1e-8–10     L2 regularisation (log-uniform)
    gamma             0–5         minimum loss reduction for a split

  The TPE sampler (Tree-structured Parzen Estimator) builds a
  probabilistic model of which parameter regions are promising after
  ~15 random trials, then concentrates sampling there.

  The best hyperparameters and the Optuna CV MAE are written to
  xgb_best_params.json. On every subsequent run the file is loaded
  directly and the Optuna search is skipped entirely.

STEP 6 — Train the final model
  The final XGBoost model is trained on 85% of the full training set,
  with the remaining 15% held out as a validation set for early stopping
  (30 rounds patience). Sample weights from Step 4 are used. The best
  iteration (determined by early stopping on the held-out validation
  split) is reported. The full 5-fold CV MAE with early stopping is
  then recomputed and reported as the primary model quality diagnostic.

STEP 7 — Assemble the LLZO feature row and predict
  The composition features, defect proxies, and live BVSE features are
  combined into a single feature vector. No inline transformations are
  applied — all BVSE values are inserted at their raw eV/Å scale,
  matching the representation used during training. LLZO's
  bvse_available = 1 (real MP CIF, no imputation).

STEP 8 — Convert and report
  The log-scale prediction is back-transformed:

    sigma = 10 ^ log10(sigma)

  An uncertainty range is estimated by shifting the log prediction by
  ±1 CV-MAE and back-transforming:

    sigma_low  = 10 ^ (log10(sigma) − CV_MAE)
    sigma_high = 10 ^ (log10(sigma) + CV_MAE)

  CV-MAE is used here rather than training MAE because it reflects
  held-out generalisation error, giving a more honest bound. The
  experimental reference range for cubic LLZO is ~10^-4 to 10^-3 S/cm.

DIFFERENCES FROM GBT_predict_LLZO.py
--------------------------------------

  Model           GradientBoostingRegressor → xgb.XGBRegressor
  Hyperparams     Hardcoded → Optuna-tuned, cached to xgb_best_params.json
  Early stopping  None → 30 rounds on inner 85/15 split (CV + final fit)
  CV evaluation   cross_val_score → manual KFold with early stopping
  Uncertainty     ±1 train-MAE → ±1 CV-MAE (held-out, not training error)
  Defect features charge_compensation_proxy removed
  BVSE derived    bvse_energy_inv added (1/Ea; Arrhenius-motivated)
  Sample weights  Identical scheme (real=1.0, proxy=0.5, guessed=0.1)


KEY DEPENDENCIES
----------------
  pymatgen     — Composition parsing, ionic radii, oxidation states, CIF I/O
  mp-api       — Materials Project REST client (structure fetch)
  bvlain       — Bond-Valence Site Energy and void distribution
  xgboost      — XGBRegressor with native sample-weight and early stopping
  optuna       — Bayesian hyperparameter search (TPE sampler)
  scikit-learn — KFold, train_test_split, mean_absolute_error
  pandas/numpy — Data wrangling and feature construction
